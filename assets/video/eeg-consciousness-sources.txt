When Consciousness Is Lost · Scalp EEG

Young-Tak Kim, Hayom Kim, Mingyeong So, Jooheon Kong, Keun-Tae Kim, Je Hyeong Hong, Yunsik Son, Jason K. Sa, Synho Do, Jae-Ho Han and Jung Bin Kim.
Differentiating loss of consciousness causes through artificial intelligence-enabled decoding of functional connectivity. NeuroImage 297 (2024), 120749. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2024.120749

The researchers compared three causes of loss of consciousness in 150 retrospectively studied patients, with 50 patients per group. They calculated scalp EEG coherence from expert-selected, artifact-free 20-second epochs across 19 channels and five frequency bands. The three conditions were focal-onset nonconvulsive status epilepticus, metabolic encephalopathy, and benzodiazepine intoxication.

In ten-fold cross-validation, the CNN achieved 88% accuracy and AUC 0.905 (Table 1). In prospective testing, 25 of 30 participants were correctly classified. That count is the diagonal sum of Figure 6A (6 + 9 + 10). The comparison applies to the three conditions studied. Broader clinical generalization needs independent evaluation.

The head illustrates a reference scalp montage separate from the study participants. The wires carry individual electrode signals. In the separate network diagrams, lines illustrate statistical relationships between scalp recordings. The reported numerical results come from the published study.
